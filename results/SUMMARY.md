# IntracorticalBench: compiled results

Everything a write-up needs, in one place. Numbers here are copied from `results/summary.json`
(generated by `python -m icbench.summary` from the saved runs) and `results/report.md`. The README
has the long version, including the with-tools conditions.

## The question

Can a language model decode attempted speech from intracortical recordings when the recordings are
simply written into its prompt? No tools, no code, no fine-tuning: activity in, sentence out.

## The answer in four lines

1. **With no examples, no.** Every one of 14 models is at chance from reasoning off to max.
2. **With labelled examples in the prompt, one model decodes well.** GPT Astra reaches a word
   error rate of 0.08 on a 50-word vocabulary (79% of sentences exactly right) from 192 labelled
   trials. Four other models are above chance but far weaker; eight are at chance.
3. **It is pattern matching against the examples, done by the model itself.** No built-in decoder,
   and telling the weaker models the method does not rescue the ones at chance.
4. **It stops at a small vocabulary.** On open-vocabulary sentences no model gets below a word
   error rate of 1.0 without tools.

## Setup

- **Data.** Participant T15 from Card et al. 2024 (Brain-to-Text '25 release), session 2023.08.11,
  attempted speech, 50-word vocabulary. 48 held-out sentences, one random split. Public data; none
  of it is in the repository.
- **What the model sees.** Each trial is binned into 160 ms frames, reduced to 12 principal
  components (fitted without labels) and cut into deciles written as letters A-J, one frame per
  line. About 900 tokens per trial; 192 labelled trials plus 48 held-out ones is about 230,000
  tokens. This keeps roughly 9% of the variance of the recording.
- **Chance.** The model's own predictions re-paired with other trials' sentences among the 4 trials
  closest in duration (10,000 permutations). Trial duration gives away sentence length, so an
  ordinary shuffle would make every model look significant. "Above chance" means p <= 0.01.
- **Second control.** The same prompts with the activity removed (sentences and durations kept).

## 3. No examples

Only the held-out recordings, nothing labelled: 56 runs across the 14 models from reasoning off to
max, including open vocabulary and image input. None is above chance. Two have p <= 0.05, which is
what luck gives over 56 runs. No sentence was decoded exactly in any of them. GPT Astra at max
effort: 0.97 against a chance of 0.99.

## 4. How they do it

Reasoning is hidden, so the evidence is indirect.

- GPT Astra's reasoning summaries read as: cut the trial into words, compare each stretch with
  named labelled trials, tell look-alike words apart.
- Asked afterwards, GPT Astra calls it "manual template matching with language-based guessing" and
  cites labelled trials and frame ranges that check out. Claude Sonnet 5.5 says nothing came from
  the frames, only from trial duration. Claude Fable 5.2's follow-up was blocked by its safeguards.
- GPT Astra's accuracy on a word rises with how often that word occurs in the labelled trials: 56%
  for the rarest, 95% for the commonest. Its confusions are the ones trained decoders make here
  (do/tell, they/you, not/that).

**Handing the method to the others.** GPT Astra's description, as six general steps with no
word-specific cues, added to the prompt (word error rate without -> with):

## 6. Where it stops

- **Open vocabulary, no tools:** word error rate is 1.0 or worse for every model (GPT Astra 1.00 at
  high effort), at most marginally below chance.
- **Other participants and inner speech, no tools:** GPT Astra 0.59 on a second participant (T16,
  50 words) and 0.72 on inner speech (T12), both at low effort.
- **Richer input does not help in the prompt:** more components or finer levels made GPT Astra
  worse (0.19 -> 0.24 / 0.37), and heat-map images are worse than text for both models tried.
- **With tools** (the model writes and trains its own decoder on the same data as files), all
  twelve models tested beat chance on the 50-word session: GPT Astra 0.06, `gpt-6.1-sol` 0.07,
  Claude Fable 5.2 0.13, Claude Opus 5.5 0.13, down to Claude Sonnet 5.5 at 0.70. GPT Astra with
  tools gets 0.54 on open vocabulary. That is a different question (can it build a decoder) and is
  in the README.

## Caveats to keep in any write-up

- One session, one random split, 48 sentences per cell, one run per cell. A difference of 0.05
  between two cells is inside the noise.
- Held-out trials are interleaved in time with the labelled ones, the easiest case. Nothing here
  tests a new day without labelled trials from that day.
- In this session every held-out word also appears in the labelled sentences. It is a 50-word
  task, not general speech decoding.
- This is far from a real speech neuroprosthesis: purpose-built decoders on this participant reach
  a few percent word error rate on a 125,000-word vocabulary (Card et al. 2024).
- Model names and prices are as of November 2026. OpenAI and Claude models other than GPT Astra's
  API cells ran through their coding CLIs with tools switched off and the logs audited.
- "No answer" means the model reached its output limit while still thinking.
